## 2. Findings

**F1 - BLOCKER - Braced-bay base anchorage does not work with the loads properly placed.** The report removes the wall base shear from the column bases ("goes to the slab through the anchored wall rail"). That is not the statics of the system: BoardX spans on girts between columns, a pinned-pinned column delivers wL/2 to its base plate whatever is bolted along the floor; the rail only carries the strip below the lowest girt. Re-running the designer's own anchor check with V_wall restored (same direction as the bay shear, e.g. S wind at K21 gives both) and keeping their H/2 strut share and uncracked V_Rd,c = 48.1: K21 interaction **1.38** (V 48.4 kN, edge alone 1.01), K23 1.02, K22 1.01, K19 0.95. With the cracked edge value (F2): K21 2.07, K23 1.55, K22 1.47, K19 1.24, K17 1.03, K27 1.03, K14 1.01. Without a proven strut (F3) the compression base takes H + V_wall: K21 74 kN vs 34 kN (2.2). The bases of B3, B5, B7, B8 are therefore 1.4-2.2 x over with the binding basis values. Fix: redesign the braced-bay bases so the shear is not carried by post-installed anchors near the solid-zone edge - through-bolts with a plate under the slab, a grouted shear lug in a cored pocket, or 2-3 more bays per direction so that H + V_wall <= ~25 kN per base; then re-run the interaction with V_wall included for all 27 bases.

**F2 - MAJOR - Cracked/uncracked inconsistency.** Cone uses k = 7.2 (cracked) but edge shear uses k9 = 2.4 (uncracked). The anchors sit in the top of a slab over a column head - the hogging tension zone - so cracked applies: V_Rd,c = 34.0 kN, not 48.1.

**F3 - MAJOR - Base strut sharing H/2 is unproven.** Load sharing between two anchor groups through an HEA 160 bolted to side gussets in 2 mm clearance holes is governed by slip, not by the strut's stiffness; one group takes the full H until it displaces 2 mm. Either fitted/injection bolts or welded ends with a compatibility check, or design each base for H. The struts also add 1.26 t and floor-level obstructions (bay B8 is inside the hall).

**F4 - MAJOR (basis, both alternatives) - Horizontal component of the roof suction is missing.** Uplift acts normal to the 3.43 deg roof: 614 kN x sin 3.43 deg = 36.8 kN char towards north, i.e. +34 % on the S-wind roof force (108.7 -> 145.5 kN). B8: T 68 -> 92 kN (angle 0.48, still OK), N_col 45.5 -> 61 kN, K17/K21 uplift 60 -> 76 kN (cone 0.66). "Friction included in 1.3" is also wrong (0.8 + 0.5 = 1.3 without friction; friction adds ~6 kN, negligible). Feeds F1.

**F5 - MINOR** - Correct the basis text (theta = 0 = wind from N, low eave, F -1.7/G -1.2/H -0.6; theta = 180 = from S, -2.3/-1.3/-0.8). No effect on C.

**F6 - MINOR** - Gutter loads (0.25 / -0.50 kN/m, 0.05 kNm/m) are in no script. Effect on members < 2 %, but they must go on the eave rail, fascia and the 0.6-0.7 m rafter cantilevers. Coordination: drainage puts the west gutter at y 35.37, the structural model roofs and walls the whole north edge at y 35.87 (columns K5-K7 at 35.17-35.27, eave beam 35.22, 0.65 m overhang). Confirm the west-block north edge.

**F7 - MINOR** - Column interaction uses k_zy = 0.6 k_yy (Table B.1) while treating the column as LTB-susceptible; Table B.2 gives k_zy ~ 0.98: K25 0.79, K23 0.76, K22 0.62 (report 0.68 / 0.64 / 0.56). HEA 160 still adequate.

**F8 - MINOR** - Purlin uplift: report says "<= 6.5 kNm elsewhere"; the same routine gives 8.3 kNm on the 3.07 m corner spans (x 92.5-95.6, zone F, y 35.6 and 20.6). 8.6 kNm on the 4.07 m stair strip is 0.68 of a gravity capacity; the free-flange uplift capacity of Z200x2.0 is typically 55-70 % of it, so these spans probably need a mid-span anti-sag row and supplier confirmation before order.

**F9 - MINOR** - Seismic: 76 kN from the roof mass alone vs 108 kN E-W wind ULS ignores floor amplification through the existing building (EN 1998-1 4.3.5-type S_a up to ~0.6 g / q_a). A two-mass check once the building period is known; bracing has margin, bases (F1) do not.

**F10 - MINOR** - Roof-diaphragm deflection not checked. RT-E rods at 0.72 give ~20 mm lateral at the east wall mid-length (h/165-180) plus 4 mm bay sway - close to h/150. RT-E/RT-W are computed as single 5.65 / 4.1 m deep panels with 10.8 / 8.6 m rods, but the drawn rods sit in 3.07 / 2.05 m cells: rod force ~84 kN in 9.7 m rods (lower, fine), but table geometry, rod lengths and weight need correcting. Consider M24 on the east band.

**F11 - MINOR** - Geometry: primary bottom at y 35.77 = 2.996 m (4 mm under the 3.0 m clear); rafter/primary bottom-flange clearance 8.5 mm nominal, 3.7 mm at the down-slope flange tip - rolling tolerance eats it. Set the primary top at TOS + 0.05 and TOS(35.87) = 3.30. Trimmers T1 (y 35.37) and T2 (y 24.16) are centred on the opening edges (half a flange over the opening); R82 at x 81.85 lies inside the brief's elevator limit 81.99 (over the shaft wall) - client to confirm.

**F12 - MINOR** - Cap-plate nuts (gauge 90, pitch 140) sit under the passing rafter bottom flange (135 wide, 20 mm above): fix the erection sequence (primaries bolted before rafters) or pitch >= 200.

**F13 - MINOR** - Reactions table: no concurrent N-V pairs per case, WP1 base missing, ULS-4 missing, V_wall to be merged into V (F1); add anchor layout and required solid-zone size for the slab check.

**F14 - MINOR** - Angle bolt bearing uses e2 = 35; the standard 40 gauge on a 70 leg gives e2 = 30 -> 124 kN for two bolts, B8 0.55 (with F4 0.74). OK.

**F15 - MINOR** - Simplicity: three hot-rolled sections hold, but the lateral system is 8 X-bays + 8 floor struts + wall-rail anchors + 20 rod-braced roof panels (40 rods, sag ties, gussets) + fly braces + tie plates; 23.6 t is +15 % on the scheme and 1.3 t of it is struts of doubtful value (F3). Delete the struts if F1 is solved by through-bolting.

## 4. Verdict

**NOT ACCEPTABLE** in the submitted form. The superstructure (IPE 330 / IPE 270 / HEA 160, L70x7, M20 rods) is sound and would be "acceptable with fixes" (F4, F7-F12), but the load path from the braced bays into the existing hollow-block slab fails by a wide margin once two unjustified assumptions (wall shear to the rail, strut sharing) and one inconsistency (uncracked edge factor) are removed. That is the one thing this scheme must get right on an existing slab.

**Five biggest risks:** (1) anchor edge/cone failure at the 16 braced-bay bases (F1-F3); (2) the 800 x 800 solid zone is an assumption - cores before any anchor design; (3) N-S wind under-estimated by ~34 % in the basis (F4) - also affects A; (4) purlin/girt uplift capacity of Z200x2.0 unconfirmed on the 4.07 m and corner spans (F8); (5) diaphragm flexibility and BoardX drift on the east face (F10), seismic amplification (F9).

**Fitness for this client.** The pinned post-and-beam frame is genuinely simple in its members and connections, and every gravity load lands centred on a concrete column. Its weakness is structural, not architectural: it concentrates the entire lateral load into eight bays, i.e. sixteen small anchor groups near the edge of an assumed solid zone in a hollow-block slab, and the designer has already had to add a bay, floor struts and a wall-rail anchorage to make the numbers close. For an occupied restaurant with an unknown slab, an alternative that spreads the base shear over many bases with low uplift, or that anchors through the slab where access is available, is the safer choice. C can be made fit only with a base detail that does not rely on post-installed anchor shear/cone resistance, or with roughly twice the number of braced bays; the client should see this before choosing between A and C.
